# Market Sizing Data Sources

This is a discovery map, not an endorsement of specific estimates, report titles, prices, or access terms. Product coverage, costs, availability, and data definitions change. Verify current details on each provider's official page and record the date checked. Never cite a report that was not opened or whose title and claim cannot be confirmed.

## Primary public data

- **U.S. Census Bureau** — https://www.census.gov/ : business counts, economic census, demographics, and spending datasets. Check table definitions, release dates, and geographic coverage.
- **Bureau of Labor Statistics** — https://www.bls.gov/ : employment, wages, and industry series. Check NAICS scope and reference period.
- **SEC EDGAR** — https://www.sec.gov/edgar : public-company filings. Segment revenue is not automatically the same as a market total.
- **Data.gov** — https://www.data.gov/ : catalog of U.S. government datasets; inspect the originating agency and metadata.
- **OECD Data** — https://data.oecd.org/ ; **World Bank Data** — https://data.worldbank.org/ ; **Eurostat** — https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat : international datasets with source-specific units and coverage.

## Company and industry sources

Public company annual reports, investor relations materials, regulator filings, trade associations, and academic work can provide useful inputs. Verify the source's methodology, period, geography, definition, and whether it measures revenue, spend, users, GMV, or another quantity. A company's revenue or a trade association estimate may cover only a subset of the market.

Commercial research providers and databases (including Gartner, Forrester, IDC, Statista, CB Insights, PitchBook, Euromonitor, Nielsen, Mintel, LinkedIn, ZoomInfo, Crunchbase, BuiltWith, Similarweb, and others) may offer relevant research subject to licensing or access restrictions. This list does not confirm that any particular report, metric, price, or dataset exists or is currently available. Confirm the exact report/page and terms directly with the provider. Do not bypass access controls or reproduce restricted material.

## Primary customer research

Interviews, surveys, CRM records, paid pilots, and observed purchase behavior can test customer counts, needs, and willingness to pay. Record sampling frame, sample size, dates, question wording, response bias, and whether results are stated intent or observed behavior. Survey and panel vendors change plans and prices; consult official current pricing pages rather than relying on static estimates.

## Source quality checklist

For every material input, capture:

- Publisher and direct URL or user-provided origin
- Publication date and data reference period
- Unit, currency, geography, and market definition
- Methodology, population/sample, and known coverage limits
- Access status and date actually checked
- Evidence class: observed fact, user-provided data, assumption, or calculation

Prefer primary sources and independent corroboration. Two sites repeating one estimate are not independent. Reconcile definition differences explicitly and use ranges where appropriate. Mark inaccessible claims as unverified; do not invent citations or source metadata.
