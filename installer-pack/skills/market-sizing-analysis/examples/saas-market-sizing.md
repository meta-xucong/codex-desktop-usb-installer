# Hypothetical SaaS Market Sizing Example

> **Synthetic teaching example.** Every count, price, percentage, and calculation below is invented for arithmetic demonstration. This is not market research and must not be cited as evidence.

## Definition and assumptions

A hypothetical subscription product serves a defined subset of businesses. Assume 10,000 potential customers and an annual price of $1,200 per customer solely to illustrate bottom-up arithmetic.

## TAM

- Hypothetical potential customers: 10,000
- Hypothetical annual revenue per customer: $1,200
- TAM = 10,000 × $1,200 = **$12,000,000 per year**

## SAM

Assume, only for this synthetic exercise, that 40% meet the product, geography, and channel filters.

- Serviceable customers = 10,000 × 40% = 4,000
- SAM = 4,000 × $1,200 = **$4,800,000 per year**

The 40% filter is an assumption, not a researched fact.

## Capacity-based SOM scenarios

Assume a hypothetical go-to-market model can acquire 100 active customers by Year 3 and 200 by Year 5, with the same annual price. These counts should be replaced by a pipeline, conversion, staffing, retention, and delivery-capacity model in real work.

- Year 3 SOM = min(100 reachable customers, 4,000 SAM customers) × $1,200 = **$120,000 ARR**
- Year 5 SOM = min(200 reachable customers, 4,000 SAM customers) × $1,200 = **$240,000 ARR**

This SOM is derived from stated synthetic capacity, not a generic share of SAM.

## Evidence ledger to use in a real analysis

| Input | Value | Type | Required evidence before decision use |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Potential customer count | 10,000 | Synthetic assumption | Dated source, definition, geography, and deduplication method |
| Annual price | $1,200 | Synthetic assumption | Current pricing evidence or validated willingness-to-pay research |
| Serviceable fraction | 40% | Synthetic assumption | Explicit filters and customer-level counts |
| Reachable customers | 100 / 200 | Synthetic assumption | Pipeline, conversion, sales capacity, churn, and delivery constraints |

Do not reuse the numbers as benchmarks. Document source dates, units, geography, definitions, and access status for every real input.
